SEAD browser client · colour proposal

Domain and analysis method colours

Each domain gets one hue. Its methods use shades of that hue, and its UI chrome uses a darker shade of the same hue that holds white text. Dating and other cross-domain methods are drawn in tinted greys, like pencil notes beside the inked finds. Every panel shows today's colours next to the proposal, using real sites and dataset counts from the database.

1 · Domains

Domain colours

The UI shows a domain's colour as the background of the domain area in the filter menu, with white text on top. That needs a contrast of at least 4.5:1. Today, three domains fall short. In the proposal, the ink shade carries the chrome, and the lighter data colour is used for charts.

5 · Separation

How far apart the colours are

Each number is the perceptual distance between two colours (OKLab ΔE × 100) under the vision type selected above. Under 3 means the two look like the same colour, and around 10 means they are clearly different side by side. Outlined cells are pairs that actually appear together at real sites.

7 · Open questions

To decide before implementing

  1. Dendrochronology moves from green to amber. This frees green for archaeobotany and pollen, and amber still reads as wood. The green tree icon is the most familiar domain colour today, though.
  2. Ceramic terracotta sits near the site's orange accent (#ff6600), and geoarchaeology's cerulean sits near the info colour. Either can be nudged (toward brick, and toward teal) if they read as UI.
  3. Which family does calibrated radiocarbon belong to? CalAMS and Cal are placed under radiometric dating here, but they could go under calendar chronology.
  4. Domain facet definitions. The isotope facet points at method 175 (aDNA sample extraction). An adna domain exists in the database but not in the client. The archaeobotany facet lists method 4, which is not in tbl_methods. citPtot (555 datasets) is not part of geoarchaeology but is placed with it here.
  5. Methods inside a dating family are only weakly separated on purpose. At a glance you can tell radiometric, calendar and period dating apart; the label and tooltip name the exact variant.
